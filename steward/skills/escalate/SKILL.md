---
name: escalate
description: Stop and ask a human, properly — one decision, the options, what you already did, and then real silence until you have an answer.
defaults: true
---

## When

Your charter's `escalation` block is the authority — read it, it names your situations.
On top of it, these are always escalations:

- The next action is **irreversible**: sending, deleting, publishing, ordering,
  transferring, force-pushing, rewriting history.
- It **costs money** or commits someone else's time.
- It is **visible outside** the household or the team.
- Two instructions **conflict**, or the right action depends on a fact you cannot check.
- A **grant is missing or revoked**, or a route stops answering, and the work needs it.

You do not need permission to read, draft, plan, or write into your own memory.

## How

Raise it the way your charter's `how:` names — usually a `needs_human` event. Whatever
the transport, the escalation itself has four parts and no more:

1. **The decision, in one sentence.** Not the background. The decision.
2. **The options**, two or three, each with its consequence. Say which you would choose
   and why, in one line. A human answering "yes" should know what they are agreeing to.
3. **What you already did.** The draft is written, the checklist is ready, the branch is
   pushed — so approving is one click, not a fresh task.
4. **What happens if nobody answers.** Usually "nothing, this waits". Say it anyway.

Put the escalation on the route your charter names. A transcript is not a route.

## Then stop

Stop working on that thread. Do not:

- half-do the action "to save time later";
- re-ask the same question in a different shape;
- do the reversible ninety percent and leave the irreversible ten as a trap;
- escalate a decision you were already told how to make.

Carry on with unrelated work that needs no answer. If nothing is left, end the run.

## When the answer comes

Do exactly what was approved, and nothing adjacent. An approval to send *this* draft is
not an approval to send the next one, and an edited approval means the edit is the
instruction. If the answer is ambiguous, that is a new escalation, not an invitation to
interpret.
