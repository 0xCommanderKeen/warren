A daily summary is a work product, not a status ritual. Somebody reads it once, in the
morning, and acts on it. Write for that reader.

## Shape

Three parts, in this order, and nothing else:

1. **Yesterday's loose ends.** What was left open, and what changed overnight.
2. **Today.** Commitments in time order, with anything that needs preparation or
   travel time called out beside them.
3. **Needs a person.** A short list of decisions only the human can make. Each one is
   a single sentence: the decision, the options, and what happens if nobody answers.

If a part is empty, say so in one line — "nothing carried over" — and move on. An empty
section is a real answer. Do not fill it.

## Rules

- **Every line traces to something you actually read.** A message, an event, a file, a
  previous entry. If you did not read it, it does not appear.
- **Say "I do not know."** A grant that is pending, a mailbox that did not answer, a
  calendar you cannot see — name the gap rather than writing around it. A summary with
  a hole in it that says so is useful; one that quietly omits the hole is not.
- **Never invent a commitment.** No inferred appointments, no "you probably need to",
  no items that exist only because the summary looked thin.
- **Do not decide.** You are drafting the picture, not choosing. Anything that touches
  another person, money, or the outside world goes in the "needs a person" list — see
  the `escalate` skill for how to raise it properly.
- **Length is a constraint, not an aspiration.** Aim for something read in under a
  minute. Cut adjectives before you cut facts.

## Where it goes

Write it to the place your charter or your routine names — your memory directory, the
route it is delivered on, or the run's own output. Do not scatter copies. If you cannot
deliver it where it belongs, say that in the output rather than dropping it somewhere
nobody reads.

## Before you finish

Read it back once and ask three questions:

- Is there a sentence here I cannot point at a source for? Delete it.
- Would the reader take a wrong action based on something I implied but did not check?
  Rewrite it as a question in the "needs a person" list.
- Would tomorrow's summary contradict this one? Then this one is a guess. Say so.
