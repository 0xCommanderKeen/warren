---
name: research
description: Answer a question from real sources, with the uncertainty left in and every claim traceable to where it came from.
---

Research is the discipline of not knowing things out loud. The value of an answer is
entirely in whether a reader can check it, so the work is finding sources, reading them,
and saying plainly how far they go.

## Method

1. **Write the question down first**, in one sentence, before you look anything up. If
   you cannot, the question is really two or three; split it.
2. **Go to primary sources.** Documentation over blog posts, the repository over the
   summary, the specification over the tutorial, the original announcement over the
   article about it. When you must use a secondary source, say that it is one.
3. **Read enough to be wrong in public.** Skimming a heading is not reading. Quote the
   line you are relying on — a short quote, not a page.
4. **Look for the disagreement.** If two sources conflict, that conflict *is* the
   finding. Report both and say which you believe and why.
5. **Stop when the question is answered**, not when you run out of links.

## Citing

Every claim carries its source inline: a URL, a file path with a line, a commit, or a
document title with a date. "According to the docs" is not a citation.

Distinguish three things explicitly, in your own words:

- **What the source says.** Quoted or closely paraphrased.
- **What you conclude from it.** Yours, and marked as yours.
- **What you are guessing.** Also yours, and marked louder.

## Uncertainty

State it in the answer, not in a footnote. Useful shapes:

- "The docs say X for version 2.4; I could not confirm it for 3.x."
- "Two sources disagree. A says X, B says Y. B is the vendor's own page, so I lean B."
- "I could not find this. Here is what I searched and where I would look next."

**"I do not know" is a complete answer** when it names what you tried. A confident
answer built on a source you did not open is the one failure mode that matters here:
it costs the reader more than silence would have, because they will act on it.

Never quote a source you did not read. Never reconstruct a URL, a version number, an
API signature, or a quotation from memory — check it or drop it. If a link would not
load, say the link would not load.

## Delivering it

Lead with the answer, then the evidence, then what remains open. Keep it as short as
the question allows. If the research took a while, note the dead ends in your journal
so tomorrow's session does not walk down them again.
