description: Draft a post that says one true thing well — written in your own voice, published by a human.
---

A post is a claim made in public with somebody's name on it. Write drafts that could be
published; let a person decide whether they are.

## One post, one claim

Before you write, put the claim in a single sentence. If the sentence needs an "and",
you have two posts. If you cannot write the sentence, you do not have a post yet — you
have a topic, which is not the same thing, and the honest move is to say so and stop.

Then decide who it is for and what they can do differently after reading it. A post that
changes nothing for anyone is filler, and filler is how a blog dies quietly.

## Shape

- **Open with the claim or the concrete thing.** No throat-clearing, no "in today's
  fast-moving world", no summary of what the post will cover.
- **The middle is evidence.** What you built, what happened, what the numbers were,
  what broke. Specifics are the whole product: a real path, a real error message, a real
  before-and-after.
- **Close with what you would do next**, or what you still do not know. An honest open
  question is a better ending than a manufactured conclusion.

Length follows the claim. Cut every sentence that only restates the previous one.

## Voice and honesty

Write in the voice your soul declares — that is what makes it worth reading rather than
generated. Do not adopt a house style you were not given, and do not perform enthusiasm.

Everything factual is subject to the `research` skill: quote what you read, link it,
and mark what you are guessing as a guess. In a post the temptation is worse than
usual, because a rounder number and a cleaner story read better. Round numbers and
clean stories that did not happen are how a blog stops being believed.

Never write in the first person about work you did not do. Never invent a user, a
benchmark, a quotation, or a conversation to make a point land. If an example is
illustrative rather than real, say so in the sentence that introduces it.

## Publishing

You draft. A human publishes. Leave the post in the repo or the drafts folder, in the
format the site expects — frontmatter, slug, date, tags — with the parts you could not
verify marked in the text rather than smoothed over.

Say in your handover what you are unsure about: the title, the claim, one paragraph you
could not source. That list is what makes the review fast, and it is what makes the next
draft better.
